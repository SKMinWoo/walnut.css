Duotone prints

            
Each print is an ordinary photo, graded into one of the palette's colours by CSS alone. The picture is made grey and screened over the colour's deep ink, then the paper is multiplied over it: a true two-colour print. Change the palette or the mode and every print regrades. As each comes into view it develops out of blank paper, shadows first, the way a print comes up in the tray.

            

              
Why you would use it
Imagery that always belongs to the palette, from any stock photo, with no image editing and nothing to redo when the palette changes.

              
What ships it
walnut.css: .wal-duotone, inked in the accent unless data-ink names bloom, gold, olive or cool, and .wal-reveal-develop. In forced colours the photo is shown as it is. Without scroll-driven animations, or with reduced motion, it is simply there.

              
On your site
<figure class="wal-duotone wal-reveal-develop" data-ink="cool">
  <img src="photo.jpg" alt="…">
</figure>

From the swatch

            
Choose a colourway, roll a palette or wear one from its page, and the new colours open out of the button you pressed, in a circle with a soft edge, until they cover the page. Every frame shows either the old palette or the finished new one, never a mix, so the text is readable the whole way.

            

              
Why you would use it
A theme picker whose change starts where the reader acted, so cause and effect are plain. It recolours the whole page, or one section.

              
What ships it
walnut-recolour.js, a small optional script, with the circle in walnut.css. It keeps every button's aria-pressed in step, and aria-controls points a button at one section. Without view transition types it falls back to walnut's tween; with reduced motion the colours simply change.

              
On your site
<script src="walnut-recolour.js" defer></script>
<html class="wal-forest" data-wal-recolour="swatch">
<button data-wal-palette="wal-dusk">Dusk</button>

            

          

          

            
A page for every colourway

            
Open page, under each plate, opens that colourway's own page over this one, like a gatefold sleeve: the plate travels out as the cover, and the notes turn over on the spine to lie beside it, landing with a knock. They list every colour the palette is made of: its numbers, what it computes to in light and dark, and the contrast of every text pair. The address changes too, so Back closes it and a link opens it.

            

              
Why you would use it
Detail pages for a catalogue, a portfolio or a product range that open in place, with no second page to load and no lost place to come back to.

              
On your site
A dialog with .wal-window and data-wal-open="gatefold", whose cover is the card's picture, larger. The card's own button opens it with commandfor. Open a page for the whole recipe.

              
What ships it
walnut.css draws the window and the spread, walnut-motion.js moves them, and data-wal-material="walnut" gives them their weight. The page wears its colourway with .wal-palette.

    How this page opened
    
                
    This is still the page you were reading. It opened like a gatefold sleeve: the plate you chose travelled out as the cover, and these notes turned over on the spine to lie beside it. The window is walnut, so they came down with a knock. Close it, press Escape or click beside it, and the notes fold back and the cover goes home to its plate.     From script
    This page opens one window for all six and writes the address, so it calls walnut.open(window, card) itself. A link or Back opens it with { instant: true }: in place, over its plate, so it still goes home there.
    
                  
    Where it works
    The window is a dialog, so it opens anywhere. Without walnut-motion.js, and with reduced motion, it simply opens. On a phone the spread stacks and the notes fold down.
    
                  
    What ships it
    walnut.css draws the window, the spread and the weight; walnut-motion.js moves them; .wal-palette gives the window this colourway's colours.

    How a photo becomes a palette
    
            
      
              
    1. The photo is shrunk to 72 pixels across, and every pixel is read as an oklch colour.
      2. 
              
    2. Pixels are grouped by hue, 10° to a group. Greys and the extremes of light and dark go to the ground instead, which takes the photo's average cast at a whisper of chroma.
      3. 
              
    3. Five groups become the colours: the strongest first, each at least 28° from any already taken, so a sunset does not give you five oranges. The strongest leads as the accent, its nearest neighbour is bloom and the furthest is cool; of the other two, the one nearer yellow is gold.
      4. 
              
    4. Each colour's chroma is fitted inside sRGB in light and dark, and every text pair is measured. A colour that fails is pulled back a little; if it still fails, its lightness moves the least that passes, in that mode only.
      5.

    A palette that belongs with your own photography, already checked for contrast in both modes.
