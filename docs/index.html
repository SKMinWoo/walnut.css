How a photo becomes a palette

                  
    
                    
  1. The photo is read at 72 pixels across, each pixel as an oklch colour.
    2. 
                    
  2. Pixels are grouped by hue, 10° to a group, leaving out greys, very light or dark pixels, and groups under 0.4% of the photo. The ground's faint tint is the average of every pixel.
    3. 
                    
  3. The strongest group becomes the accent. Up to four more are taken in order of strength, each at least 28° from any already taken, and the rest are filled in around the accent. The nearest to the accent becomes bloom, the furthest cool, and of the other two the one nearer yellow is gold.
    4. 
                    
  4. Each colour is kept inside sRGB and every text pair is measured. A colour that fails is made less vivid, or its lightness moves the least amount that passes.
    5.

Squircle and bevel corners show in Chrome and Edge. Other browsers keep round corners.

          ReferenceTokens, colour forms, cascade layers, naming, scripting and browser support
          
    
            
    
              
    Tokens you can set
    
              
    
                
    TokenControls
    
                
                  
    --wal-hue, --wal-chromaThe ground: every neutral
    
                  
    --wal-<cue>-hue, -chromaEach of the five colours
    
                  
    --wal-<cue>-inkThe colour as text, not as a fill
    
                  
    --wal-<cue>-soft, -fgA pale tint of the colour, and text on the colour
    
                  
    --wal-cue-1 to --wal-cue-5The five colours by position, for looping over them
    
                  
    --wal-l-*-light, -darkThe lightness of each surface, in each mode
    
                  
    --wal-cx-*Each surface's chroma, as a fraction of the ground's
    
                  
    --wal-elevation, --wal-washShadow depth, and the tinted light on the page
    
                  
    --wal-line-boostHow strong hairlines are
    
                  
    --wal-radius and its variantsCorner radius, per kind of component
    
                  
    --wal-corner-shapeRound, squircle or bevel corners
    
                  
    --wal-open-duration, --wal-open-easeHow openings move, with matching close tokens
    
                    
              
    
            
    
            
    
              
    Fills and text use different colours
    
              
    A colour that works as a fill is often too light to read as text. Each colour comes in four forms.
    
              
    
                
    TokenUseExample
    
                
                  
    --wal-goldThe colour as a fill
    
                  
    --wal-gold-inkThe colour as textgold text
    
                  
    --wal-gold-softA pale tint, for behind textsoft
    
                  
    --wal-gold-fgText on the fillon gold
    
                    
              
    
            
    
            
    
              
    Lower the chroma when you move a hue
    
              
    Each hue has a limit on how vivid a screen can show it, called the gamut, and past it the browser shows a duller, shifted colour without warning. At 62% lightness a chroma of 0.17 fits reds and violets but not yellows or blues, so when you move a hue far from its default, lower its chroma too. Random palette and the photo tool do this for you. The sliders don't.
    
            
    
            
    
              
    Your CSS wins
    
              
    walnut puts everything in cascade layers, and CSS outside a layer beats every layer whatever its specificity, so your own rules override walnut without !important. A rule replaces only the properties it sets, and walnut's reduced-motion rules use !important so they still win.
    
              
    @layer reset, tokens, base, layout, components, finish, utilities, cinema;

/* not in a layer: beats all of the above */
.wal-card { border-radius: 3px; }
    
            
    
            
    
              
    Naming
    
              
    wal-text-* sets a font size. wal-color-* sets a colour. There is no .wal-text-muted class: that is a token name, and used as a class it does nothing.
    
              
    <p class="wal-text-sm wal-color-muted">small and muted</p>
    
            
    
            
    
              
    Opening things from a script
    
              
      
                
    • Doors and windows are dialogs, so commandfor and closedby open and close them with no script, and focus, Escape and find-in-page come from the browser.
      • 
                
    • data-wal-open picks how a window opens. data-motion on the page, or data-wal-material on a part, picks how things move.
      • 
                
    • walnut-motion.js is optional. Without it every window still opens.
      • 
                
    • Your own movement: walnut.open.styles.mine = { open(dialog, from) {…}, close(dialog, from) {…} }, then data-wal-open="mine".
      • 
                
    • From a script: walnut.open(dialog, button) and walnut.open.close(dialog).
      • 
              
    
            
    
            
    
              
    Browser support
    
              
    light-dark() sets the floor: Chrome and Edge 123, Safari 17.5, Firefox 120. Newer features are added where the browser has them.
    
              
    
                
    FeatureNeedsWithout it
    
                
                  
    Text on each colour (-fg tokens)Relative colour syntaxFirefox 120 to 127 gets a fixed value
    
                  
    Palette changes that animate@property, Firefox 128The palette changes at once
    
                  
    Dialog and popover entrances@starting-style: Chrome 117, Safari 17.5, Firefox 129They appear at once
    
                  
    Page-to-page transitionsCross-document view transitions: Chrome 126, Safari 18.2A link just navigates
    
                  
    Recolour from the buttonView transition types: Chrome 125, Safari 18.2The colours fade across instead
    
                  
    Scroll reveals, sticky header shadowScroll-driven animations: Chrome 115, Safari 26The content is simply there
    
                  
    Menus that open by their buttonAnchor positioning from a popover's button: Chrome 133, Safari 26The menu opens in the middle of the screen
    
                    
              
    
              
    Chrome and Edge have the newest parts today. Each falls back to something that still works:
    
              
      
                
    • ::details-content: accordion rows open at once.
      • 
                
    • commandfor: open doors and windows with your own showModal().
      • 
                
    • interestfor: tips stay hidden, so keep what matters in the button.
      • 
                
    • appearance: base-select: the system's own select.
      • 
                
    • ::scroll-marker: a carousel is a row you swipe.
      • 
                
    • corner-shape: corners are round.
      • 
                
    • @function: --wal-ink() uses the fallback declaration you put before it.
      • 
                
    • :target-current: the nav needs its .is-active class.

    How colours are derived
    
        
    You set three colours and walnut makes five. Drag the accent round the wheel: bloom and cool follow it.
    
        
    
          
          
          +27°
          +209°
          olive
          gold
          cool
          bloom

          33°accent
        
    
        
    
          
          
        
    
        
      
          
    • Accent: the hue and chroma you set. Links and the main button.
      • 
          
    • Bloom: the accent plus 27°. Washes and highlights.
      • 
          
    • Gold: the hue and chroma you set. Rules and markers.
      • 
          
    • Olive: the hue and chroma you set. Tags and quiet states.
      • 
          
    • Cool: the accent plus 209°, nearly opposite it. The contrast colour.
      • 
          
    • Neutrals: the ground hue at nine lightness steps, from page to text.
      • 
          
    • Each colour keeps its lightness when its hue changes, so text contrast stays about the same.
